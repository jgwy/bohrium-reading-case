							24 heading Value: 2, 1. Does the paper support applying sctransform to both inputs (10x UMI counts and a Smart-seq2 TPM matrix)?
								25 text 1. Does the paper support applying sctransform to both inputs (10x UMI counts and a Smart-seq2 TPM matrix)?
							26 text What the paper states. The method is explicitly scoped to UMI count data, not to any continuous or read-level matrix. The Abstract says, "Our approach can be applied to any UMI-based scRNA-seq dataset" (p. 1, Abstract). The Results restate the same boundary: "our procedure can apply widely to any UMI-based scRNA-seq experiment" (p. 8, Results). The model itself is built on integer molecule counts with a negative-binomial GLM: "We explicitly model the UMI counts for a given gene using a generalized linear model" (p. 11, Methods), using per-cell sequencing depth (sum of molecules) as the covariate. The Discussion likewise frames it as "normalization and variance stabilization of single-cell UMI datasets" (p. 10, Discussion).
							27 text The paper's five validation datasets are described as "five UMI datasets from diverse tissues, generated with both plate- and droplet-based protocols" (p. 2, Results) — so "plate-based" here still means plate-based UMI protocols, not full-length non-UMI libraries. The words "Smart-seq2" and "TPM" never appear anywhere in the paper. The only reference to non-UMI data is about a different tool, not sctransform: "SCnorm [6] reached similar conclusions in the normalization of non-UMI-based single-cell RNA-seq data" (p. 3, Results).
							28 text What follows from the scenario. A 10x UMI count matrix is UMI-based, so it falls within the paper's stated scope. A Smart-seq2 TPM matrix is not: TPM is a continuous, length-normalized value, not integer UMI counts, and Smart-seq2 is a full-length (non-UMI) protocol. The negative-binomial GLM in the Methods assumes count observations and a per-cell total-molecule depth covariate, which a TPM matrix does not provide.
							29 text What remains unknown. The paper is silent on whether TPM (or raw Smart-seq2 read counts) could be coerced into the model and still be valid; there is no empirical demonstration for full-length/non-UMI data. So the paper supports sctransform for the 10x UMI input, but it does not support applying it to the Smart-seq2 TPM input.

							35 heading 3. Can Pearson residuals be interpreted as comparable expression levels across genes?, Value: 2
								36 text 3. Can Pearson residuals be interpreted as comparable expression levels across genes?
							37 text What the paper states. Yes, in the specific sense of a variance-stabilized, standardized scale — not as absolute abundance. The Results say: "We utilize the Pearson residuals (response residuals divided by the expected standard deviation), effectively representing a variance-stabilizing transformation (VST), where both lowly and highly expressed genes are transformed onto a common scale" (p. 5, Results). They are described as "effectively standardized expression values" (p. 6, Results), and the figure caption states that non-variable genes have residual variance "peaks at 1" (p. 7, Fig. 4). They are also stated to be usable downstream: "Pearson residuals are independent of sequencing depth and can be used for variable gene selection, dimensional reduction, clustering, visualization, and differential expression" (p. 5, Results).
